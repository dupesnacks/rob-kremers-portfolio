## Safe (Cosmetic) Claims

These are defensible under FTC cosmetic guidance for topical skincare products. Use freely in product copy, listings, landing pages, and marketing.

| Claim | Category | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| Helps skin look smoother | Appearance | Non-therapeutic, visual language |
| Improves the appearance of fine lines | Appearance | Cosmetic claim, not "reduces" or "eliminates" |
| Moisturizes and supports a youthful-looking complexion | Moisturizing | Compound claim okay if both parts defensible |
| Helps skin feel soft, hydrated, refreshed | Feel/sensation | Tactile claims are safe |
| Supports healthy-looking skin | General wellness (appearance) | "Healthy-looking" not "healthy" |
| Improves the look of skin texture | Appearance | Focus on appearance, not function |
| Lightweight hydrating copper peptide serum | Product description | Factual ingredient/format claim |

## Borderline (Requires Legal Review Before Use)

Claims that toe the line between cosmetic and drug. Do not use without ChatGPT + attorney approval.

| Claim | Risk Level | Why | Alternative |
|-------|-----------|-----|-------------|
| Boosts skin's natural collagen appearance | Medium | "Boosts" implies function | "Supports the look of..." |
| Promotes youthful radiance | Medium | "Promotes" can imply biological effect | "Helps skin appear..." |
| Peptide-powered skin renewal | Medium | "Renewal" implies repair/regeneration | "Peptide-infused hydration" |
| Improves fine lines and wrinkles | Low-Medium | Compound claim; "improves appearance of fine lines" is safe, wrinkles riskier | Split into separate claims |

## Prohibited (Drug/Medical Claims — Never Use)

These trigger FDA drug classification. Never include in any draft without explicit Rob + ChatGPT + attorney written approval to pivot to drug positioning (which changes the entire business model).

| Claim | Why Prohibited | Example Risk |
|-------|---|---|
| Stimulates collagen | Biological mechanism claim (drug language) | FDA action, relabeling, recall |
| Repairs/regenerates skin | Tissue regeneration = drug definition | FTC enforcement + FDA involvement |
| Heals wounds or skin damage | Medical claim | Immediate drug reclassification |
| Treats scars, acne, eczema, rosacea, or inflammation | Disease/condition claims | Drug definition per FDA |
| Reduces inflammation | Biological mechanism (systemic effect) | Drug language |
| Reverses aging | Anti-aging benefit claim (implied mechanism) | FTC scrutiny + drug border |
| Rebuilds tissue or skin structure | Tissue repair = drug definition | Reclassification + enforcement |
| Medical-grade peptide therapy | Therapy language = drug framing | Inherently medical positioning |
| "Ozempic face" treatment | Disease/condition treatment claim | Drug positioning |
| Tightens loose skin after weight loss | Tissue tightening = mechanism (drug territory) | Gray area but high risk |
| Injectable/research peptide framing | Any reference to peptide research, injectables, or clinical use | Positions as pharmaceutical, not cosmetic |
| Clinically proven or clinically tested | Clinical language implies drug-level evidence | Can trigger drug classification |
| FDA-approved or FDA-compliant | Cosmetics aren't "approved"; can imply drug status | Misleading claim |

## Enforcement Rule

If any draft copy, landing page, Amazon listing, email, influencer brief, or ad contains a **Prohibited** claim:
1. OpenClaw rewrites it immediately using Safe alternatives.
2. Flag the original violation in `DECISIONS.md` with date and context.
3. Never send the violating version for review without explicit approval to explore drug positioning.

**Exception:** If Rob + ChatGPT decide to pivot to **prescription drug positioning** (requires FDA clinical trials, IND application, Rx-only distribution), mark this file as superseded and create a new regulatory framework. This is a different business model and requires separate legal/strategic review.
